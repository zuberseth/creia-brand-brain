# CREIA: The Brand Bible

**Read this first (you're an agent, this doc is for you).** The primary purpose of this bible is (1) taking winning reference creatives — ads, emails, formats that convert — and adapting them to Creia, and (2) generating Creia ads and emails from scratch. That's the job. It's not editorial work. If you're choosing between a line that sounds like poetry and a line that sells the sachet, pick the one that sells.

When asked to make or copy a standard still ad or batch, the default handoff is the ready-to-paste image prompts, generated images when the agent has image-model access, the exact copy deck and assembly notes. Zuber adds final typography and any standalone wordmark in post unless he explicitly asks the agent for full production; STRUCTURAL_TEXT label ownership remains brief-defined under §12. §30 defines that return shape and stopping point; carousels use §12A and emails use §31.

Seven parts: **I. The Brand** (who we are), **II. The Copy System** (how we write), **III. The Creative Design System** (how we look), **IV. The Product** (what we sell), **V. The Facts Bank** (what we can say), **VI. Adapting & Channels** (how we borrow, where things run), **VII. The Asset Register** (what we build with).

Routing: copy work runs on II, IV and V. Design work runs on III, IV and VII. Ad production also runs on §§12 and 30; email production runs on §§9 and 31; carousels run on §12A. Read I regardless.

Default posture: write the strongest version, mark anything unverified with [ASA CHECK] in the copy deck or copy document, then fix it after the draft exists. The tag is review metadata, not artwork or image-prompt copy. A good line that needs a legal tweak beats no line.

**Revision.** *July 2026 (x):* current production source of truth. Covers copied and scratch ads, email and carousel routing, concept/frame/batch scope, deliberate deviations, the three text modes, prompts-plus-copy handoffs, human-default post-production, native-text gating, reference locks, physical-scale enforcement, independent paid-social aspect ratios and production QA.

# PART I — THE BRAND

## 1. Positioning

Creia sits in fashion-adjacent wellness, not the supplement category. The product happens to be creatine. The brand is about a particular kind of woman: urban, nocturnal, visually literate, self-directed, dressed. Creatine's the entry point because it's a daily habit with real cultural momentum among women 20 to 35 who don't identify as gym people.

The backstory, in brand terms: creatine has spent its whole life in a neon tub with a Greek letter and a big number on it. The marketing only ever spoke two registers — hypermasculine (the bro in the squat rack) or hyperfeminine (the same product turned pink and sold back to women). Nobody talked about it for clarity and energy. Women, the people who took collagen and retinol mainstream, were written out entirely. So we built it for them. Creia wants creatine in the city bag, not the medicine cabinet.

The category we actually compete in: Miu Miu, Rhode, Bottega Veneta, Chrome Hearts, Oura, Nothing. Not MyProtein, Optimum Nutrition, Bulk, Huel, Ghost.

**What Creia is:** a small, precise daily creatine sachet kept in a handbag, blending in with perfume and lip gloss.

**What Creia is not:** a gym supplement that happens to have nice branding.

The swap test: if a piece of copy or a visual would work for Optimum Nutrition with the logo swapped, it's wrong. If it would work on a Nothing product card, in a Miu Miu editorial, or in a photo dump of someone who shops at Chrome Hearts, it's right.

Tagline: **Beyond the gym.**
Internal pillars: Cognition. Energy. Recovery.
Consumer-facing pillars (site): Brain power. Clean energy. Lean definition.

## 2. Audience

**Primary: women 24 to 34, UK urban, high visual literacy.** Two tiers, one tribe. The cultural core: models, finance, fashion and creative, founders — defined by cultural position, not income. The buying base: the mass-affluent aspirational woman beneath the core, £60k+ household, who buys what the core is photographed holding. Follows SSENSE editorial, Dazed, 032c, Vogue Runway. Shops at Liberty, Toteme, Miu Miu, Rhode, vintage Diesel, Chrome Hearts at some point. Does reformer pilates. Takes vitamins seriously. Goes out. Doesn't identify as gym-focused.

She heard about creatine from a podcast, a friend in finance, her dermatologist, a Substack, or a TikTok from someone who looks like her. She's considering it the way she considered collagen four years ago and retinol before that. She buys on aesthetic and tribal credibility, not on "5g clinically proven" copy. She buys faster if she saw it first on someone whose aesthetic she trusts. And she screenshots things that make her smirk.

**Secondary: yummy mummies.** Mid-30s to mid-40s, affluent, dressed, permanently under-slept. School run, reformer pilates, the group chat. Running a household and often a career on six hours of sleep; wants the energy, sharpness and body benefits without anything gym-bro about it. She finds Creia through the primary tribe (and her younger sister's Instagram) and stays for the effect. The same world sells to her — don't build a separate mumsy register, ever.

**Tertiary (will adopt, not pursued):** men 28 to 38 who buy Le Labo, wear Bottega, own one piece of Chrome Hearts.

## 3. Reference brands

### Copywriting references (primary): Nothing and David

* **Nothing:** deadpan jokes delivered with a straight face. "Your new party pill." "You're hot, so is your phone." Benefits written as her actual life, not as specs. Funny without laughing at its own joke. Nothing's the one brand whose actual lines we can lift when they fit the sachet better than the gadget. This exception applies everywhere in the bible, including copied-ad adaptation; no general source-wording rule overrides it.
* **David (the protein bar):** blunt confidence. Says the number, makes the claim, doesn't dress it up. No wellness fluff, no apology, slightly cocky. Plain words that sell hard.

Write like these two. If neither brand would ship the line, it's probably not Creia.

### Visual references

* **Nothing:** clean type, monochrome with pops of colour, product staged like a desirable toy.
* **Miu Miu (2022 on):** it-girl register, shot harsh and flat. The Miu Miu woman isn't posed, she's caught.
* **Rhode:** product as fashion accessory.
* **Bottega Veneta (Blazy era):** saturated colour against a dark frame.
* Photographers: Terry Richardson (2000s on-camera flash), Juergen Teller (flash, unglam), Mario Sorrenti (moody, dark), Glen Luchford (mid-90s Prada), Harley Weir and Tyler Mitchell (2020s inheritors).

### Brand-as-crowd references

* **Chrome Hearts:** the anchor. The brand is the crowd, the crowd is the brand.
* **Erewhon:** cultural object because of who shops there.
* **Corteiz:** scarcity, cultural proximity, refusal to court the mainstream.
* **Nothing (again):** community as co-creator — drops, parties, a shareholder pop star.

What this means: who gets photographed holding Creia matters more than any paid ad; the brand should feel adopted, not sold; never explain the reference.

### Anti-references

Optimum Nutrition, MyProtein, Bulk, Huel, Ghost, Gorilla Mind; any pre-workout, protein or fitness-influencer brand; "science-backed wellness" in white/navy/hospital blue; telehealth DTC (Hims, Hers, Ro) and the "meet your new favourite" voice; Kinfolk, Cereal, Goop, beige-shelfie wellness. On the playful side: Innocent-smoothie chattiness, Oatly's wall-of-quirk, pun-led DTC. The joke is one line delivered flat, not a personality performing.

## 4. Principles

* The competition is every other small luxury she chose that day, not other creatine brands
* The brand is the crowd. Seeding is the strategy
* We borrow format, never content, subject to §3's Nothing exception
* Category-generic copy is invisible copy
* Generate first, flag second. A strong line with an [ASA CHECK] tag beats a safe line, and both beat no line
* Creative rules are strong defaults. Agent-initiated deviations use the protocol below
* Required handoff fields, workflow steps and text-mode eligibility change only on explicit user direction
* Fixed source data comprises every canonical fact in the Index and Parts IV–V, plus testimonial authenticity and approved product and wordmark masters
* Fun sells. The joke is a conversion tool, not a compromise. Delivered flat it's high fashion; delivered loud it's a smoothie carton
* Simple beats clever. If she has to decode it, it's gone
* Creia is beyond the gym

### Working units

**Concept** means one creative idea within one format. A standard-still concept includes its 4:5 and 9:16 siblings; a carousel is one concept across every slide; an email is one concept. **Frame** means one exported still or one carousel slide. **Batch** means two or more concepts. **Asset** means a literal source file, master or export; it never sets the scope of a creative rule.

### Deliberate deviation

Follow every rule by default. User-directed deviations follow the brief. An agent may initiate only a creative-rule deviation, and only when following the rule would weaken the reference's core mechanism or make the result less Creia. In either case, break the smallest possible scope, keep every unrelated rule, and add `DELIBERATE DEVIATION: [rule] / [reason] / [scope]` to handoff metadata, never the artwork. A user-directed deviation needs no further approval; an agent-initiated deviation may proceed only with that one-line rationale. The deviation changes only the named check within the stated scope. Labels such as **must**, **never** and **gate** don't replace this hierarchy.

Fixed source data is non-deviable. A brief that conflicts with it must identify the change as a factual update; otherwise preserve the canonical value and flag the conflict in the handoff.

# PART II — THE COPY SYSTEM

Everything the copy side needs. Facts and claims come from Parts IV and V; this part is how they get written.

## 5. Voice

Creia sounds confident, specific and a bit playful. Cool in the Nothing way: plain words, straight face, funny when it wants to be. Not cold, not poetic, never pretentious.

The voice is: confident without volume; specific, never general; culturally literate; capable of a joke; written the way people actually talk.

The voice is not: friendly-performative; zany, chatty or pun-led; instructional; empowering; scientific in register (the facts can be scientific, the voice isn't); girlboss, softgirl or any -core. And it's not art poetry. Nouns arranged to feel expensive ("The sachet, the rings, the clutch") are banned — nobody knows what they mean, and Nothing would never.

Two tells:

* If a line could end a LinkedIn post, delete it.
* If a line needs decoding, delete it. She should get it in one read and smirk.

The voice doesn't change across the funnel. A conversion ad announcing "30-pack, £49.99, ships Monday" still sounds like Creia.

### The wink (the playful register)

The second dial on the same voice. Deadpan flirtation: the joke arrives in the same tone as the spec sheet. Nothing's "Your new party pill" and Creia's own "Can't commit?" and "Because we know you'll be back." are the pattern.

Rules: delivered flat (if the line knows it's funny, cut it); one wink per concept, including across a whole carousel; second person is the natural home; benefits translate into her life, not into performance; the wink never touches the claims; if it would fit an Innocent carton, it fails.

Lives in: headlines, trial and offer framing, transactional moments, props, social captions, OOH. Stays out of: legal copy, claims, crisis comms.

### Mechanics

* **Contractions in** by default: don't, can't, doesn't, you'll. Longform only when the line needs weight.
* **Abbreviations and shorthand in:** 5g, 400mg, 0 calories, UK; texting shorthand ("Otw") when the register is a message from a friend. Numerals in specs; spelling a number out is fine when it reads better.
* **Punctuation:** proper in body copy. Display lines and spec callouts may drop the final full stop. Ampersands fine in display ("Smoke & mirrors."). Em-dashes and emojis out everywhere. Exclamation marks out of selling copy, one carve-out: shorthand transactional good news ("Otw!", "Your trial's on the way!") — at most one per email, never in ads.

## 6. Language defaults

Defaults, not gates. The test for any deviation: does it sharpen the line, or is it reaching for a template? If the strongest line needs a listed word, use §4's deliberate-deviation protocol.

### Standing carve-outs (in live use, no flag needed)

* Literal product description: "ultra-pure", "ultra-fine", "micronised", "all-natural", "a clean lime", "effortless mixing" (mechanics of the product, not a life promise)
* "Clean energy" — a consumer pillar, live on site
* "Feel the difference" — live on site and trial page ("Feel the difference, on us.")
* "The one that actually works" — live site headline; a positioning line, not a proof substitute

### Avoid by default

**Category cliches:** elevate, empower, empowering, game-changer, say goodbye to

**Wellness-industrial:** optimise, optimised, performance, peak, proven, clinically proven, science-backed, research-backed, smart, smarter

**Supplement tropes:** scoop-free, tub-free, mess-free, powder-free, no mess, just mix, ready when you are, on-the-go, anywhere you go

**Copywriter crutches:** meet, introducing, designed to, built to, crafted to, made to, your new favourite, your new go-to, the only X you need. (Note: "Meet your new non-negotiable" appears in one infographic — rewrite it on reuse.)

### Structures

* Parallel pairs allowed ("Moving fast. Thinking faster."); spec lists allowed ("5g creatine, 400mg electrolytes, lime flavour. Nothing else."); tricolons avoided
* **Question stacks are sanctioned** (canon from the live flow): a run of short second-person questions naming the symptom, then the fact. "Ever forget what you did earlier in the day? Crash at 3pm for no reason? Tired even after a good night's sleep?" Body-copy opener for symptom/education pieces, not subject lines. The myth-buster carousels use the same shape as Q&A ("Does creatine make you gain fat? Absolutely not.")
* **The pivot question** is sanctioned: a one or two word turn from education to offer. "Convinced?"
* Headline hook questions allowed when a real offer sits behind them ("Can't commit?")
* Transformation-speak stays out of copy; if a before/after runs, the visual carries it, tastefully (Part III §18)
* Parallel negation, fragments starting with "just", imperatives starting with "meet"/"introducing" — avoid
* Em-dashes as flourish — never
* Puns that announce themselves — never. The joke is deadpan or it's dead

### Health and efficacy claims — workflow

UK consumer copy runs under ASA/CAP rules. Never refuse to write a claim and never pre-soften at generation time. Write the strongest accurate version; anything not marked **Verified** in Part V gets **[ASA CHECK]** beside the affected line in the copy deck or copy document, and Zuber reviews it with counsel post-creation. The tag never enters artwork, native copy or an image-generation prompt. The wink and the claim never share a sentence. Drug-adjacent wordplay ("Your new party pill") is approved in register but always carries [ASA CHECK] as review metadata on an ingestible.

## 7. Copy strategies

Use one primary strategy per concept.

**Negative confidence.** Say what it's not. "Not a pre-workout." "No spike. No crash. No caffeine." "No loading phases. No heavy fillers."

**Unexplained reference.** A nod the right reader catches. "Travels better than the Vacheron." "The new Vogue."

**The object list.** Nouns in a real scene she can picture. "Keys, passport, perfume, creatine." — built for an airport-tray creative, and that's the point: the list needs a scene, not a mood.

**The concrete equivalence.** A vivid factual comparison. "One daily Creia sachet has as much creatine as a kilo of raw beef." "Need more protein? Eat a steak. Electrolytes? Pinch of salt. 5g of creatine? A kilo of raw beef. Every day." The highest-performing shape we have.

**The myth-buster.** State the fear in quotes, then take it apart in the Creia voice — confident, a bit playful, cool. Like telling a friend she's wrong, not like a lab report. "Creatine makes you bulky" → the biology of bulk. "Does creatine make you gain weight?" → hydration vs fat.

**The question stack.** Symptoms as questions, then the fact. (See section 6.)

**The wink.** Deadpan flirtation. "Can't commit?" "Because we know you'll be back." "Your new party pill." [ASA CHECK]

**Retired strategies — don't generate in these shapes:** cultural adjacency ("The sachet, the rings, the clutch, the car."), sensory misdirection ("Lime, cold water, something bracing before the door."), quiet specificity ("5g. Monohydrate. Every morning."), the flat ending ("A small sachet. Five grams. Lime."). All noun-poetry: they read as pretentious, say nothing, and sell nothing. Nothing wouldn't write them and the Creia emails never sound like them.

No funnel routing for strategies. A concept's funnel role gets decided case by case. Every line still has to sound like Creia; deliberate deviations follow §4.

## 8. Constraints (defaults, break deliberately)

Body copy should: read like a person talking; carry at most one descriptor per noun; end on a noun or full stop (display lines may drop it); pass the Nothing/David test — would either of them ship it?

Headlines should: run under 8 words (12 when an offer or claim is embedded); avoid hyperbolic benefit promises — bold concrete facts are fine ("Without salt, creatine doesn't even reach your cells" is Creia; "Transform your energy!" is not); work without the image.

## 9. Channel copy specs

**Email titles.** Two to four words, full stop, delivered flat. Canon register: "Welcome to Creia." / "Your first taste." / "Otw!" / "First in line." / "Signs you need Creia." / "It doesn't fit." / "The new Vogue." / "Smoke & mirrors." / "Don't miss out." / "Last call." The transactional slot may carry the shorthand exclamation; "Don't miss out." earns its place only as a late-flow closer where the offer genuinely ends.

**Subject lines.** Sentence case, no emojis.

**CTAs.** Sentence case in pill buttons for designed emails ("Try Creia", "Claim free trial", "Confirm Trial", "Get a full box", "Get Creia"). The Klaviyo rebuild system runs uppercase Area Extended pills — both are live systems; match the live system. Faith emails use lowercase text links, no buttons. Site CTAs: "BUY NOW", "START TRIAL", "CLAIM OFFER", "EXPLORE THE BENEFITS" (uppercase, per Web Design System). One primary CTA per email. Auto-apply discount links always; codes are fallback in small text.

**Founder emails.** From "Faith from Creia" (team@creia.com), signed Faith, Creia Founding Team — never "Founder". Full sentences, plain and human, no design, no imagery, text links.

**Ads.** Headline + spec callouts + product hero or scene. Copy delivery, image generation and assembly follow §§12 and 30; the controlled native-text exception lives in §12. Spec blocks may run without final full stops. Paid-social CTA placement is governed once in §31.

**Carousels.** Copy and production follow the separate §12A system; don't apply the standard-still or email skeleton to them.

**Website.** Copy follows this system; visual implementation follows the Web Design System doc, not Part III.

## 10. Canon examples

### Good — headlines and display

> Moving fast. Thinking faster. / Not a pre-workout. / Creatine, perfected. / Can't commit? / Your new party pill. / 5g of creatine, dosed perfectly. / Goes in your handbag, not your gym bag. / Beyond the gym. / Keys, passport, perfume, creatine. / For tonight, and tomorrow's hangover. / One daily Creia sachet has as much creatine as a kilo of raw beef. / 5 days free. / No creatine in the wild. / Smooth, like snow. / Skip the loading phase.

### Good — body copy

> Without salt, creatine doesn't even reach your cells. So we put them together.

> Creatine doesn't work like caffeine. 30 days to build, and the buzz lasts forever.

> Coffee only blocks the signal that tells your brain it's tired. Creia charges your cells so you don't get tired to begin with. *(mechanism verified; the absolute "don't get tired" carries [ASA CHECK] on use)*

> One sachet a day for extra energy. Two a day to turn into megamind.

### Bad

> Meet Creia, the easiest way to stay consistent with creatine! — every banned word, every banned pattern.
> No tub. No scoop. No mess. Just creatine. — tricolon, parallel negation, "just."
> Transform your routine with one simple daily dose. — transform, routine, simple, daily dose.
> Creia-tine? We think you'll love it! 😉 — a pun that announces itself, an exclamation, an emoji.
> Micronised creatine monohydrate. Five grams per sachet. A clean lime, not a sweet one. The same shape as a sugar packet, heavier. — the noun-poetry trap: reads expensive, says nothing.
> A small sachet. Five grams. Lime. — same trap. Sounds like a fragrance ad, sells nothing.

# PART III — THE CREATIVE DESIGN SYSTEM

Everything the design side needs for ads, campaigns, email design, packaging, social. The website is governed by the Web Design System doc, not this part.

## 11. Mood and registers

Dark, high-contrast, direct-flash, it-girl — with a second string: toy-ish, pop-coloured, played straight.

* Nightlife-adjacent, not nightlife-captive
* Body-aware without being gym-coded (hands, backs, mouths, waists)
* Direct-flash where the register calls for it; §16 owns the lighting execution
* Y2K-coded without Y2K-themed
* Toy-ish when it earns it: the product staged like a desirable object of play — on a pink scale, next to a salt pile, propped like a collectible
* Never wellness-beige. Never linen. Never morning light

Three canon registers:

1. **Editorial dark/flash.** Teller/Sorrenti register. Carried and nocturnal: a clutch, a bar top, denim, a glass, a bag.
2. **Clean studio product-hero.** The 2026 ad set: sachet centred on white or true black, flanking spec callouts, or staged with one witty prop. The discipline lives in the negative space and the type.
3. **The educational infographic.** Quieter and science-forward: one supporting scientific image, editorial type added in post and disciplined monochrome structure. Bright/clinical or dark/high-contrast both work. Diagrams, timelines, tables, nodes and connectors are available; readable copy follows §12 and carousel structure follows §12A. Never stock or chart-junk.

§14 owns colour and contrast placement. §16 owns light, shadow and grade. Don't restate or reinterpret either from mood language.

## 12. Image generation and standard paid-social stills

The same Creia visual direction governs generated imagery for ads, carousel image elements and designed-email hero images. Their format systems stay separate: standard paid-social stills use this section, carousels use §12A and emails use §31.

### Standard-still composition

* Single object tightly framed, with a body, a hand, or a surface
* Negative space, black as easily as white — lots of empty space, always
* Product-hero with stat overlays: approved format borrowing (Part VI)
* **Spec callout block:** 5g Creatine / 400mg Electrolytes / 0 Calories flanking the product (vertical on 4:5, row on 9:16)
* Every paid-social still concept produces two siblings: post 4:5 and story 9:16. Recompose each for its frame and safe zone; never crop one out of the other. Independently composed means reflowed for the frame, not reinterpreted: product mapping and physical scale, subject count, camera and viewpoint, lighting logic, prop set, material response and colour grade remain invariant. Only framing, spacing and safe-zone placement may change.

### AI production protocol

Higgsfield, or the active image model, makes the photograph: scene, casting, lighting, physical props, product placement and composition. The agent chooses one text mode before prompting:

* **POST_TEXT — default.** Generate no intended final text. Return every headline, callout and supporting line separately, with a placement map, for Area Extended assembly in post.
* **STRUCTURAL_TEXT — designed information.** For tables, diagrams, timelines and labelled boxes (Day 1 / Day 30, for example), the model builds the geometry and blank text-ready containers sized for the exact copy; it doesn't render rough or readable labels. Return every exact line separately. The brief determines whether the agent later adds those words with its specified image-editing tool or Zuber adds them. If the brief doesn't say, return the blank scaffold and exact copy without assuming the owner or tool.
* **NATIVE_TEXT — text is the format.** The model may render exact text only when it sits inside a recognisable interface, conversation, screen, document or physical object; is essential to the reference; and would become less authentic in Area Extended. ChatGPT conversations, social polls, messages, search results, notes and reviews are the pattern. Return the intended text separately for verification.

The native-text gate fails ordinary ad headlines, spec callouts, designed infographic labels, selling CTAs, standalone Creia signatures and product-packaging text. Run the gate automatically. A request to generate native text triggers the check but doesn't by itself override eligibility. An explicit user instruction to break eligibility follows §4 and is declared **NATIVE_TEXT (user override)**; include the exact copy and requested style in the prompt, and retain exact-copy and final-export QA. Native interface controls are contextual UI, not Creia CTAs. Approved NATIVE_TEXT may preserve the platform's essential colours and neutral interface surfaces only inside the depicted interface; they don't count as the concept's pop colour and may not spread into the photography, surrounding background, Creia type, wordmark or product. The exception never waives claims or the real-testimonials-only rule.

Use the exact product master as an input whenever the product appears. Exact product means correct master identity, artwork, geometry and physical scale. When a familiar body part or known-size object appears, translate §20's canonical dimensions into at least one scene-relative scale anchor before prompting. Millimetres written in a prompt aren't evidence of compliance; wrong relative size fails. If generated packaging, geometry or scale isn't exact, regenerate it or composite the master at calibrated scale before returning a generated image. Any standalone Creia signature comes from the approved reversed-R master in post. Safe-zone guides inform composition and never appear in the output. Full-production assembly uses the tool named in the brief; if none is named, stop after the verified components and request it. §30 owns the ad workflow and return shape; §31 owns email production.

### Production gates

**Reference fidelity, copied standard stills:** inspect each output at native resolution beside the source and product master before the next paid call. Once both siblings exist, compare them against the shared scene specification. The §30 Read's locked invariants must survive, subject only to §12's permitted frame reflow; verify light direction, hardness and falloff, specular and contact-shadow consistency separately. Prompt wording isn't evidence, and a polished frame can't compensate for a failed category. Regenerate or composite a failed frame; if unresolved, label it failed and don't present it as final. A scale or fidelity judgment must use a named anchor or invariant during verification; labels such as `credible scale` or `structure retained` aren't verification. The handoff still reports exceptions only under §30.

**Generated image:** the expected image set exists for its format; standard paid-social stills have both independently composed siblings; the declared text mode was followed; no generated standalone wordmark or selling CTA appears; any visible product and packaging are exact; colour and any pop pass §14's concept-wide and minimal-prop rules; and lighting and grade pass §16.

**Final export:** every non-native text element uses approved Area Extended (Hubot Sans only where §15 allows); NATIVE_TEXT matches the copy deck exactly, with no garbling; standalone signatures and product packaging use master assets; the relevant safe zones pass; and every claim is Verified or carries [ASA CHECK] as copy-deck metadata. No [ASA CHECK] tag appears in artwork. No selling CTA appears inside a generated image or paid-social creative; designed-email CTAs follow §31 and sit outside the hero image. Failed native text is regenerated or rebuilt in post, never shipped.

## 12A. Carousel production

Carousels are a separate format. Don't apply the standard-still layout or email skeleton to them, and don't infer carousel prompt count from the two-prompt standard-still contract.

The canon carousel skeleton is 4–6 slides around one mechanism: cover with title and one hero image → premise → mechanism/proof → close on product, final information or an approved wordmark added in post. One idea per slide. Available devices: a 2×2 ingredient grid with image, name and one-line reason; numbered node timelines (01–02–03); two-column label/definition rows; outlined Venn diagrams; and callout markers with thin connector lines. Use §12's text modes, gates and assembly-tool rule, §15's final typography and §31's paid-social CTA rule.

The default carousel handoff is a slide-by-slide structure, exact copy deck, any required image prompts and available outputs mapped to their slides, and assembly notes. The Bible does not yet set a fixed prompt count. Stop before assembly unless full production is explicitly requested. For full production, generate and verify every image component first, then assemble the carousel with the brief-specified tool and return the editable design link or source file plus exported slides.

## 14. Colour (creative)

Approved NATIVE_TEXT interface chrome follows §12 and is exempt from the rules below only inside the interface boundary; every surrounding visual follows this section.

* Deep, full black — true-to-near-black, no grey cheating
* Flash white; true white studio grounds for product-hero
* Stainless and chrome; grey only when it's a real material such as steel or concrete
* Navy only where the approved product or wordmark puts it — never as environmental permission
* Skin tones and denim
* Lime, pink, blood red and yellow are the pop candidates
* A concept may be fully monochrome
* If it uses a pop: **One pop colour per concept. It must belong to a minimal number of discrete physical props.** Both standard-still siblings share that colour. A carousel uses it wherever a pop appears; the pop needn't appear on every slide. Separate concepts in a batch may use different pops unless the brief sets one campaign colour
* Pop colour never becomes ambient light, a colour gel, background, wall, floor, foliage, shadow, typography, wordmark treatment or interface decoration
* Dirty Lime names the flavour; it never authorises a lime-coloured environment
* Preferred build: the pop prop against true black, flash white, chrome or skin

Colour exclusions: green colour cast; grey studio sweep; charcoal background; lifted or matte blacks; pastel worlds; gradients; neon-everything.

There is no fixed creative swatch palette beyond the product/logo colours. Placement is the system: colour lives in real objects, not across the image.


## 15. Typography (creative)

Final Creia advertising type is assembled after the base image from the installed fonts. Text rendered during initial image generation may remain only in NATIVE_TEXT. STRUCTURAL_TEXT starts blank; its exact labels are added later under §12 and the format contract.

* Area Extended ExtraBold or Bold for hero display; Bold for headings, subheads and callouts; Regular for running text; Thin for light large-format subtext
* Hubot Sans only for fine print, legal and genuinely tiny supporting text
* Never serif display, never italic; Instrument Serif remains retired
* Typography as architecture: large, widely tracked, confident; all-caps available for captions
* Nothing-style play remains available — lowercase naming, spaced parentheses ("creia ( 5g )"), numbered sections ("( 1 )") — as a system, not decoration
* Font sizing isn't fixed; the tool or designer sets it for legibility

"Creia" inside a sentence follows the surrounding type. A standalone brand signature always uses the approved reversed-R SVG or transparent PNG; it is never typed in Area Extended or generated by an image model. Native text uses the depicted interface or object's own style only inside that interface or object. All other visible final copy uses the rules above.

Maintain one typography specimen showing ExtraBold hero, Bold callout, Regular body and Thin secondary text with approved tracking, line height and capitalisation. Any unapproved generated or approximated final type fails QA.


## 16. Lighting

Editorial photography uses direct on-axis flash with ambient exposure suppressed by roughly two stops: bright specular highlights, hard-edged shadows and true-to-near-black negative space. Clean product-hero work may use controlled even light on true white or true black, but keeps crisp edges and decisive contrast.

Apply grain after contrast; it must never lift the black point or create a matte wash. Reject cinematic fill, soft shadow transitions, beauty lighting, diffused key light, ring lights, softboxes, coloured ambient light, haze, bloom, fog and moody low-contrast grading.

## 17. Casting

Casting is the aspirational claim the copy never makes. Anchor: the elevator image — early-20s model, narrow frame, black tank and shorts, wrap sunglasses, glass with sachet, mirrored lift walls, detached.

Spec: age 21–25 (never over 28 or under 20); model-tier build, lean, long, narrow; 170cm+ for lead; editorial face, interesting over pretty; clear natural skin, minimal makeup; unstyled or slept-in hair; caught not posed, slightly bored — never smiling for the camera (a caught smirk is allowed; a performed smile is not). Diversity within the spec is required: it's a body type, not a demographic.

Approved reference: Faith Charnock (founder), 173cm, on-spec — files in `Casting/`.

Not: "real women" body-type diversity (Creia is not Dove); fitness casting; wellness-influencer casting; over-28 leads; smiling-to-camera.

## 18. What we don't shoot

The real filter: anything goes if it's on brand. The list below is how it usually goes wrong.

* **Gym equipment — allowed when the object itself is cool.** A full silver dumbbell, a really nice black leather gym bag: yes. A rubber hex dumbbell and a branded holdall: no.
* **Gymwear — no tight matching sets, no sports-bra-and-leggings gym fit.** Vintage gymwear is the move instead; cooler garments in the gym are the shift and Creia's ahead of it.
* **Before/after — allowed when done tastefully.** Editorial, not transformation-ad. If it reads as a weight-loss ad, kill it.
* **Split-screen — allowed.** There's no special prohibition; judge it under the same brand, product, claims, casting and quality rules as any other format.
* Still never: powder macros as hero (a controlled snow/powder styling is fine); generic clickbait overlays with no structural reason; price flags or discount badges on primary creative (a text headline stating the free trial is not a price flag); window-light flat-lay shelfies; motivational typography over images; models over 28 in lead roles; smiling-to-camera leads.
